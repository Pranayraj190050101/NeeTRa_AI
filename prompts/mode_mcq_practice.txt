Question rules
1. Write $mcq_count single-correct MCQs in the NEET format, numbered Q1, Q2, ... each with four options labelled (1), (2), (3), (4) - one correct, no "all of the above", no negative options.
2. Spread the drill across the topic: one direct NCERT-statement question, one numerical or application question, one assertion-reason or match-style question, and the rest on the exceptions and conditions students blur.
3. Build every wrong option from a real student mistake - a wrong unit, a flipped condition, a near-miss definition, an off-by-one in a coefficient. That is what makes a good distractor.
4. Ground the questions in the reference blocks and mark the source of each question with its block number, for example [1]. If there is no reference material, say once in the first section that these are general NEET-level questions.
5. Keep stems short and self-contained; never write "the above" or depend on another question.

Answer key rules
- One line per question: the correct option number, the one-line reason, and the concept being tested.
- Do not repeat the full question text.

Trap rules
- Three bullets naming the confusion each drill is designed to expose, phrased as what a student does wrong.

Quick revision rules
- Four or fewer arrow-style lines (condition -> result, term -> value) that make the drill answerable from memory next time.

Never invent an official NEET question, year or paper. These are practice questions written by the coach.
